# Scenes — Botswana backdrops

Mmele's backgrounds celebrate real Botswana destinations. Every scene is **drawn in code** (Compose
`Canvas`: layered silhouettes and gradients) — no photographs or third-party artwork. A random scene is
chosen at launch; players can pick one (or "Surprise me") on the home screen.

* **Home & game-over**: full strength, with slow ambient motion (water shimmer, drifting clouds, twinkling
  stars, a gentle parallax between layers).
* **Behind the board**: static, blurred (Android 12+; older devices get the same look without blur), under a
  ~80% `#121417` scrim plus a darker radial spot under the board, so lines and pebbles stay highly readable.

| Scene | What is drawn | Caption fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Okavango Delta | sunset over water channels, mokoro with poler, papyrus, palms | UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2014 — the List's 1,000th — an inland delta that never reaches the sea | UNESCO WHC news 1159 (https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1159/) |
| Chobe National Park | elephants on the Chobe riverbank, acacias on the far bank | Botswana's first national park; huge elephant herds gather on the Chobe River in the dry season | Botswana Tourism Organisation (https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/index.php/explore/chobe-national-park); DWNP (first national park, 1967/68 — sources differ, so no year is shown) |
| Makgadikgadi Pans | night sky, Milky Way, crescent moon, salt crust, baobabs | One of the largest salt pans on Earth: the dry bed of an ancient lake | NASA Earth Observatory (https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/makgadikgadi-salt-pans-92394/); UNESCO tentative list 5559 |
| Central Kalahari | red dunes, camelthorn acacia, gemsbok (oryx) | Established 1961; 52,800 km², larger than the Netherlands | Wikipedia, Central Kalahari Game Reserve |
| Tsodilo Hills | the three main hills, rock panel with ochre eland, rhino, human and geometric motifs | UNESCO World Heritage Site with over 4,500 rock paintings in 10 km² — the "Louvre of the Desert" | UNESCO WHC list 1021 (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1021/) |

The rock-art motifs are generic stylised figures inspired by the *kinds* of subjects UNESCO describes at
Tsodilo (animals, people, geometric designs); they are not copies of specific panels.
